## 2. Who paid whom, what buyers kept, repeat payments

- **Who paid whom:** each listing owner paid outbid.lol (Wilke) directly through Polar at launch, Dodo Payments today. No revenue share, no ads network. Wilke also used the board to promote his own supastarter and now sells adjacent placements (a backlink offer on the page) [Seen].
- **What buyers kept after losing #1:** their listing stays on the board forever at its **cumulative** amount; all-time spend "never expires" [Seen /rules]. So a loser still holds a rank (e.g. #4) and keeps receiving clicks. That is very different from our rule, where a replaced post leaves the top spot and keeps only its own page.
- **Repeat payments:** from day 1, re-entering the same URL charges only the difference to climb [Seen /rules]. Bidding wars at the top were mostly top-ups between the same few companies.
- **Why buyers shared [Estimate, supported by Startup Spells]:** being #1 was a named public event the founder reposted; the board's visible clicks gave bragging numbers; being outbid created drama for another post; and the buyers sold to the same founder audience that was watching.

## 5. Three rule sets, side by side

| | Outbid at launch (19 Aug) | Outbid today (19 Nov, /rules) | TopForNow (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spots | Unlimited ranked list | Unlimited; All-time, Today, Daily boards | **One** spot |
| Entry | $2 minimum (press release says $5) | $10 minimum, whole dollars, max $999,999 | First post free once; then **$1, $2, $4, $8…** |
| Taking #1 | Pay more than #1 | At least **$5 more** than current #1 | Pay exactly the displayed doubled price |
| Repeat | Fresh bid each time | Top-up pays only the difference | No top-ups; every replacement pays full price |
| After losing | Keeps cumulative rank | Keeps cumulative rank forever | Leaves the spot; keeps its own page, votes, results |
| Free visitor | Browse, click | Browse, click | **Vote**, browse history, results, RSS, respond |
| Refunds | None | None | None for being replaced; automatic full refund if a payment can't be published |

## 7. Our choice

- **One audience for the first global launch:** indie makers and small SaaS founders who build in public (X, Indie Hackers, Peerlist, r/SideProject, Hacker News). They already understood Outbid, they ship weekly, and they can pay $1–$64 on impulse.
- **One reason they choose us:** at the start, a few dollars buys the **whole** spot, not row #432 of 3,233, and you get a public results page and an Ego Receipt to post.
- **One homepage promise we can deliver:** *"Pay the price on the button and your post is the only thing on top until someone pays double. Everyone sees how long you held it and what it got."* Every part of that is enforced by code and shown on the results page.

## 8. How this fails even if launch attention arrives

1. **Novelty decays fast.** Outbid's own traffic fell 23% by day 4 with 75% bounce, and its Today #1 now costs $31 with $11 revenue for the day. Our doubling price climbs while attention falls.
2. **Doubling caps the game.** Round 10 is $512, round 14 is $8,192. Without a big audience, demand probably stops between $32 and $512, so total gross may be under $100 (see economics).
3. **Early rounds are almost free.** $1 and $2 sales lose ~45% and ~25% to fixed processing fees; one $30 chargeback wipes out the first five rounds.
4. **Processor risk.** Outbid hit Polar's acceptable-use limits mid-launch. Dodo's public list does not ban paid placement, but underwriting is unconfirmed (see operations doc).
5. **No audience of our own.** Outbid worked because a 19k-follower founder narrated every buyer. We start at zero, so each change of the spot must be posted by us and shareable by the buyer.
6. **Clones and abuse.** 189 clone domains in 48 hours; moderation and scams are a constant cost.
